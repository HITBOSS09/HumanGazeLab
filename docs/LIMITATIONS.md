# Limitations

1. Ten COCO-FreeView participants. The subject bootstrap describes resampled means of these ten ids. It is not a population interval for other observers. Subjects 106 and 111–115 are absent from the file, and the file does not say why.
2. The same ten participants appear in the train and validation image splits. Validation tests unseen images, not unseen people. The subject-held-out center model is the check that removes one participant from the center fit. It moved B1 from 0.870 to 0.851 bits on validation.
3. Image-cluster intervals treat images as the resampled unit. The same observers contribute to every image. On the frozen COCO-FreeView validation table those intervals are narrower than the subject intervals (B1 width 0.053 versus 0.222 bits). They are not a correction for observer dependence. A crossed resample of subjects and images, recomputing the subject-equal mean, is in `outputs/audit_checkpoint/coco_uncertainty.csv`. On COCO-FreeView its width stays close to the subject interval (B1 0.230 bits; B3 0.342 versus a subject width of 0.307). It is a sensitivity for that sampling scheme, not a mixed model and not a population interval.
4. B2 and B3 use other people's fixations on the evaluation image. They are consensus references. They are not models that could be run on a new photograph without new eye data.
5. B3 keeps one fixation per observer per index. On COCO-FreeView validation the subject-equal mean goes negative while the median number of other observers is still 9 (index 5: fixation-mean −0.131 bits, median nearest other observer 96.6 px, 27.9% of probabilities near the floor of log2(0.05) = −4.322 bits). Observer dropout is not the change at that index. By index 15 the median reference count is 5 and the median nearest observer is 168 px. A miss under this kernel and floor can be about −4.32 bits, so the mean can be negative without an indexing error. The checkpoint tests match B3 to an explicit leave-one-observer density. They do not identify why observers are farther apart at later indices.
6. Later fixation indices are the scanpaths that were still going. Restricting COCO-FreeView validation to the 3,144 scanpaths that reach index 15 does not remove the decline: B3 early-minus-late is 2.410 bits (subject interval 2.045 to 2.728). Index 1 on that subset is 1.811 bits, close to 1.773 on all 6,001 validation scanpaths. The overall B3 mean on the long subset is more negative (−0.457) because those scanpaths contribute more late fixations. Short scanpaths are not the source of the sign change. Who stops looking can still differ from who continues; this check does not measure that difference in observers, only the scores inside scanpaths that continue.
7. The frozen primary model still uses the pre-specified list whose lower edge is 24 px. Center and history selected that edge. `outputs/temporal_v2/train_fold_bandwidth.csv` scores 4, 8, 12, and 16 px on the training selection folds only. Center and the pooled leave-one-observer map do not move below 24 px. History's train-fold maximum is 16 px, by 0.0015 bits on COCO-FreeView and 0.021 bits on OSIE. That 16 px value was not scored on held-out images. On the 40 px grids, 4 px and 8 px are not distinguished. The primary published numbers remain the frozen `temporal_v1` and `osie_v1` fits.
8. The binning is 40 pixels on a side in the primary grids. Information gain is for that discrete distribution. It is not a continuous-pixel likelihood and it is not the MIT/Tübingen NSS.
9. Index is not time. `T` was not used. COCO-FreeView sessions are described as 5 seconds and OSIE sessions as 3 seconds. A shared "fixation number 9" was not analyzed.
10. OSIE durations in the file go down to 20, while the 2014 paper says fixations under 100 ms were removed. The column was not used. The conflict is open.
11. Nine OSIE JPEGs from the same code repository were overlaid with the released fixations (`outputs/audit_checkpoint/figures/osie_fixation_overlay.png`). Each file is 800 by 600, and every fixation on those nine images falls inside the JPEG. The paths sit on scene content when y increases downward. That is a check of the released image frame on nine of 700 images. The paper's scaling of those images onto a 1680 by 1050 display, including any side padding, was not reconstructed. The repository license that was retrieved is the MIT license for the software. A separate image-license sentence was not found. The JPEGs were not committed.
12. OSIE subject ids are file positions. The authors' 500/200 split was not recovered.
13. The failure atlas for COCO-FreeView has no photograph underlay. The 18 extracted images were not the images the predeclared rule selected.
14. No low-level or object features were computed. No claim about scene content, beyond the human-consensus maps, is available from this run.
15. MIT1003 raw samples were left untouched. No fixation table was synthesized for them.
16. An interval that excludes zero is not a causal claim and not a policy claim. The worksheet has no adopted hypothesis.
